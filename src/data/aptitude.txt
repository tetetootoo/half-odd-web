# how i think

Years before I knew I would end up somewhere between design and software engineering, I took a professional cognitive aptitude assessment.

Some of the strongest patterns it identified were logical reasoning, structured and systematic thinking, working memory, spatial reasoning, and the ability to recognise relationships across complex information.

At the time, they were simply test results.

Looking at the work I gravitated toward later, they make a little more sense.


## patterns that stood out

**logical reasoning**

Recognising rules, relationships, and structure - and using them to work through complex problems systematically.

**systems thinking**

Breaking something complicated into understandable parts, seeing how those parts relate, and creating structure around them.

**analytical transfer**

Recognising a pattern in one context and applying what I learned from it somewhere else.

**working memory**

Holding and working with relatively large amounts of information while solving a problem.

**spatial reasoning**

Understanding visual and spatial relationships, alongside an interest in form, structure, and how things fit together.

**technical understanding**

Quickly understanding underlying principles, technical relationships, and structured processes.


## where that shows up now

I see those patterns most clearly in the work I enjoy doing.

Understanding how a product fits together before deciding how it should look.

Finding the reusable system underneath several individual interfaces.

Moving between visual relationships and technical architecture.

Following an interaction from an idea to the code that actually makes it behave.

Taking something ambiguous apart, finding the structure inside it, and putting it back together in a way that feels simpler.

Maybe that is why neither design nor engineering on its own ever felt quite complete.


## a note on the assessment

The assessment was conducted in 2014 and combined standardized cognitive tests, practical tasks, conversation, and behavioral observation.

I don't treat it as a current measurement or a definition of what I can do.

I kept it because, years later, some of the patterns it identified are surprisingly recognizable in the work I chose.
