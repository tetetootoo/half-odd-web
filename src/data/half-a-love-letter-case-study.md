# Half a Love Letter

## Testing where AI ends and design judgment begins.

**Experiment · AI · Interaction Design · Development**

Half a Love Letter started with a small idea: an anonymous place to leave a thought, confession, or love note for a stranger to find.

I used the project to test something else I was curious about:

# How good had AI actually become at designing an experience?

---

## Three tools. Strangely similar answers.

I explored the same idea with Claude, Figma AI, and Framer.

Different tools repeatedly converged on familiar layouts, predictable components, and literal interpretations of the brief.

They were good at producing **an** interface quickly.

They were less convincing at deciding what **this** interface should feel like.

# Strangely similar answers.

So I stopped asking AI for the finished answer and started treating its output as something to react against.

---

## The final experience

<!-- FINAL_DESIGN
Full-width final design.
Follow with a close interaction crop or short envelope-opening sequence.
-->

I designed the final interaction around a hand-drawn envelope and letter.

Visitors can anonymously leave something behind or open a random letter written by somebody else.

The implementation is deliberately small: vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript with Firebase Firestore.

No framework. No elaborate architecture.

It didn't need one.

# AI shortened the distance to a possible answer.
# It didn't remove the need to decide whether that answer was any good.
